# Positioning & go-to-market

**The sentence:** "You are deploying agents. We answer the question *who did what, under whose authority, and how do you prove it* — to the board, the auditor, the regulator." Not an agent platform (giants' turf); the answer to the question that blocks their deployments.

## Adoption ladder (each rung has standalone value; nobody buys the ladder at once)

gateway (see) → gates (control) → standing mandates (safe autonomy) → Svod layer (governed memory) → Servanda (commitments between people).

## Personas

- **Employee:** feels nothing until a consequential action pings them for a signature. Their win: the agent can do *more*, not less — "allowed, but gated" replaces "forbidden, because who knows."
- **Team lead:** lives in pending queue + mandate registry; renews expiring standing rules deliberately.
- **CISO/auditor:** monthly audit explorer visit; the quarterly "what do your AI systems do and who oversees them" becomes an export button.

**The asymmetry that sells itself internally:** the product disarms the AI-team-vs-security conflict — AI team gets "yes," security gets control. Champion: Head of AI / platform engineering. Economic buyer: CTO/CISO. External clock: EU AI Act (human oversight + traceability now mandatory posture for EU orgs).

## Motion

Demo-first: Variant A (fleet story) for vision, Variant B (bring-your-own-agent, 15 min to their own audit trail) for conversion — see build plan. Path: 2–3 design partners (free/symbolic, for logo + feedback + case study) → public case study → self-hosted license.

## Pricing hypothesis (do not commit before first design-partner conversation)

Per active subject (agent identity under governance) / month, annual commitment, flat tiers for procurement predictability; support/updates as second stream. Single-tenant self-hosted; EU hosting an option, not a requirement.

## Dual use

The identical narrative is the interview asset: "here is how I would deploy agents at your org without your CISO stopping me" — with a working demo behind it. Buyer = hiring manager; nothing changes but the audience.
